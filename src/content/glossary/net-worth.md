Take a hypothetical household with $395,000 of assets: a $300,000 home, $20,000 in savings, $60,000 in retirement accounts and a $15,000 car. It owes $255,000: a $240,000 mortgage, a $9,000 car loan and $6,000 of credit card balances. Net worth is $395,000 − $255,000 = $140,000.

Lenders look at net worth, together with savings and other assets, as one sign of financial cushion next to income, debts and credit; the CFPB lists savings and total assets among the factors mortgage lenders weigh ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/does-my-credit-score-affect-my-ability-to-get-a-mortgage-loan-or-the-mortgage-rate-i-pay-en-319/)). A statement that shows net worth, such as a [personal financial statement](/glossary/personal-financial-statement/), is how you document it.

Net worth is not income, and it is not cash. Most of the example above is tied up in a house and retirement money, so it says little about how much you can spend or put toward a down payment today; that is what [liquid assets](/glossary/liquid-assets/) show. Net worth can also be negative when debts exceed assets, which is common early on, for example with student loans. Use the [net worth calculator](/net-worth-calculator/) to compute yours.
