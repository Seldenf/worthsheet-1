For a mortgage, the CFPB explains that the interest rate is the yearly cost of borrowing without fees, while the APR also reflects points, mortgage broker fees and other charges you pay to get the loan, so the APR is usually higher than the interest rate ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-a-mortgage-interest-rate-and-an-apr-en-135/)). A loan quoted at 6.00% interest might have an APR a little above that, depending on its fees (hypothetical).

Lenders must show both numbers: on a Loan Estimate you can find the interest rate on page 1 under Loan Terms and the APR on page 3 under Comparisons. APR makes it easier to compare offers that carry different fees, but the CFPB warns against comparing the APRs of fixed-rate and adjustable-rate loans casually and against using APR alone to choose a loan.

The APR is not your monthly payment rate; your payment is calculated from the interest rate and term (see [amortization](/glossary/amortization/)). It does not include [escrow](/glossary/escrow/) items such as property taxes. APR is part of a loan's [conditions](/glossary/conditions-credit/).
