According to myFICO, the five categories carry these weights: payment history 35%, amounts owed 30%, length of credit history 15%, new credit 10% and credit mix 10%. Salary, occupation and employment history are not part of a FICO score, although lenders may weigh them separately ([myFICO](https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/whats-in-your-credit-score)). In practice that means paying on time is the biggest lever.

Lenders use FICO scores widely, and there are many versions of FICO. For manually underwritten Fannie Mae loans, the minimum representative credit score is 620 for fixed-rate and 640 for adjustable-rate loans, while loans run through Desktop Underwriter have no set minimum score. Lenders can set stricter requirements ([Fannie Mae Selling Guide](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-5.1-01/general-requirements-credit-scores)).

FICO is not the only credit score: VantageScore is another widely used model, and the number you see for free may not be a FICO score. All of them start from your [credit report](/glossary/credit-report/), and a [credit score](/glossary/credit-score/) of any brand is just a summary of that data.
