After you choose a loan and proceed with the application, the lender may ask for documents to verify what you reported, then approves or denies the application ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-do-i-have-to-do-to-apply-for-a-mortgage-loan-en-144/)). The underwriter checks your income and employment, your assets for the down payment and reserves, your [credit report](/glossary/credit-report/), your [DTI](/glossary/debt-to-income-ratio/) and the property's value and condition, which sets the [LTV](/glossary/loan-to-value-ratio/).

Fannie Mae describes this as a comprehensive risk assessment covering equity investment, credit history, liquid reserves, reliable recurring income and the adequacy of the property as collateral ([Fannie Mae Selling Guide](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-1-01/comprehensive-risk-assessment)). Underwriting can be done by automated software, as with Fannie Mae's Desktop Underwriter, or manually, and the limits differ between the two.

A [pre-approval](/glossary/pre-approval/) is not underwriting's final answer: the decision comes after the full file is reviewed. If the underwriter asks for more documents, send them promptly and keep new debt out of the picture. The standard application already collects your assets and liabilities, but a dated [personal financial statement](/glossary/personal-financial-statement/) can make the asset side faster to document.
