Also called a personal financial statement, statement of net worth or personal balance sheet. All four names describe the same kind of report, so whichever one a lender or advisor uses, you are producing the same thing.

A short example, dated December 31 (hypothetical numbers):

| What you own (assets) | Amount | What you owe (liabilities) | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Savings and checking | $25,000 | Mortgage | $140,000 |
| Retirement account | $55,000 | Car loan | $12,000 |
| Home (market value) | $130,000 | Credit cards | $8,000 |
| **Total assets** | **$210,000** | **Total liabilities** | **$160,000** |

Net worth: $210,000 − $160,000 = **$50,000**.

Lenders, advisors and programs such as the SBA ask for this report to see the full picture of what you own and owe. The SBA's version is [Form 413](https://www.sba.gov/document/sba-form-413-personal-financial-statement). The statement must carry a date because values change, and many lenders want it to be recent.
