Picture two applicants with the same income. One has paid every account on time for years, and the other has collection accounts and recent late payments. The first shows stronger character, because the record suggests repayment is reliable. The record is what counts, not personal opinions.

Lenders read character from your [credit report](/glossary/credit-report/) and the [credit score](/glossary/credit-score/) built from it. In the FICO model payment history carries the largest weight, 35% ([myFICO](https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/whats-in-your-credit-score)). Business lenders may also consider your track record and references.

Despite the name, character does not measure personality or honesty in any general sense. It is a documented history of handling credit. Reading your own report before you apply, free at [AnnualCreditReport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com/), lets you spot mistakes in time.
