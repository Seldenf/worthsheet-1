Conditions describe the loan itself and its setting, not the borrower. The same $240,000 loan at a hypothetical 6% rate has a payment of about $1,438.92 a month over 30 years but about $2,025.26 over 15 years. The shorter term costs more each month, which changes how comfortably it fits the borrower's budget, even though the borrower is unchanged.

Lenders weigh the purpose (a home to live in versus an investment property, or equipment that produces revenue), the amount, the [interest rate and APR](/glossary/apr/), the repayment schedule (see [amortization](/glossary/amortization/)), and the outside environment, such as the economy and, for a business, its industry. The [FDIC's small-business lending module](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-resource-center/mssb-m5-pg.pdf) explains the five C's from the lender's side.

Conditions are the one C you cannot fully control. They are also unrelated to the legal "terms and conditions" in a contract. Use the [mortgage calculator](/mortgage-calculator/) to see how a different rate or term changes the payment. The [overview](/glossary/five-cs-of-credit/) covers all five.
