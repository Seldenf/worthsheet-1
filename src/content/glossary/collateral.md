In a car loan the vehicle is the collateral; in a mortgage it is the home. If you stop making payments, the lender has a legal right to take the asset, and for a home that happens through foreclosure. A loan backed by collateral is a secured loan, while a credit card without a pledged asset is typically unsecured.

Lenders ask how much the collateral is worth compared with the loan. For a mortgage that comparison is the [loan-to-value ratio](/glossary/loan-to-value-ratio/), and the appraisal exists to confirm the property's value. Fannie Mae's risk assessment includes the adequacy of the property as collateral ([Fannie Mae Selling Guide](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-1-01/comprehensive-risk-assessment)). Business lenders may also ask for business assets or a personal guarantee, depending on the loan.

Collateral is one of the [five C's of credit](/glossary/five-cs-of-credit/). It is not the same as a [down payment](/glossary/down-payment/), which is cash you put in, or [capital](/glossary/capital-credit/), which describes your savings and net worth. Pledged assets still appear on your statement as [assets](/glossary/assets/), with the loan listed as a liability.
