List each liability with its current balance, not the original amount. A $240,000 mortgage that has been paid down to $215,000 is a $215,000 liability. The WorthSheet template covers 13 liability types, including credit cards, mortgages, student loans and auto loans.

Lenders look at liabilities in two ways: the total owed, which reduces [net worth](/glossary/net-worth/), and the monthly payments, which drive your [debt-to-income ratio](/glossary/debt-to-income-ratio/). Fannie Mae's guide, for instance, counts installment debts and alimony or child support that extend beyond 10 months, revolving account payments, lease payments regardless of how many months remain, and the housing payment ([Fannie Mae Selling Guide](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-6-02/debt-income-ratios)).

Liabilities are debts you already owe, not everyday spending: groceries and utilities are expenses, not liabilities, unless a bill is past due. Obligations that may arise later, such as a loan you co-signed, are [contingent liabilities](/glossary/contingent-liabilities/) and are shown separately.
