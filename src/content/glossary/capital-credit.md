Two applicants want to buy a $300,000 home. One has $60,000 for the down payment and still has savings afterward. The other can put down $15,000 and has nothing left. The first has more capital: a larger stake in the property and a cushion if income dips.

Lenders see capital in your savings, [liquid assets](/glossary/liquid-assets/), [down payment](/glossary/down-payment/) and [net worth](/glossary/net-worth/). Fannie Mae's risk assessment, for example, looks at your equity investment in the property and your liquid reserves ([Fannie Mae Selling Guide](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-1-01/comprehensive-risk-assessment)). A [personal financial statement](/glossary/personal-financial-statement/) is the document that shows capital in one place.
