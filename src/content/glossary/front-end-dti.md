Example: a proposed housing payment of $1,800 a month and gross income of $6,000 a month gives a front-end DTI of $1,800 ÷ $6,000 = 30%. The housing payment normally includes principal, interest, property taxes and homeowners insurance (PITI), plus mortgage insurance and association dues when they apply, so a payment that is [escrowed](/glossary/escrow/) is counted in full.

A widely quoted benchmark is 28% for the housing ratio (and 36% for total debt, the [back-end ratio](/glossary/back-end-dti/)). It is a rule of thumb, not a universal rule: lenders and programs set their own limits, and many focus on total DTI ([Fannie Mae Selling Guide](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-6-02/debt-income-ratios)). Ask your lender whether a housing-ratio limit applies.

Note on WorthSheet tools: the Homebuyer Readiness tab of the template calculates its front-end ratio from the mortgage principal and interest payment only, so it will read lower than a lender's PITI-based figure. Add taxes and insurance when you compare. The [affordability calculator](/how-much-house-can-i-afford/) includes them. See also the [overall DTI definition](/glossary/debt-to-income-ratio/).
