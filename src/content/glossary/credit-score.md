Most credit scores range from 300 to 850, and a higher score usually makes it easier to qualify and may bring a better rate ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-credit-score-en-315/)). Scoring models weigh factors such as bill-paying history, unpaid debt, the number and type of accounts, account age, how much available credit you use and new applications for credit.

You do not have just one score. Each depends on the model and the data behind it, and may even change from day to day. Mortgage lenders use specific versions: for Fannie Mae loans, the lender requests three scores and uses classic FICO or VantageScore 4.0 models ([Fannie Mae Selling Guide](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-5.1-01/general-requirements-credit-scores)). The number in a banking or card app may be a different model and may not match what a lender sees.

A credit score is calculated from your [credit report](/glossary/credit-report/), so errors on the report can lower it. The best-known model is the [FICO score](/glossary/fico-score/). For mortgages, your score is one input along with income, debts and savings; see the [readiness assessment](/mortgage-readiness-assessment/) for how it is scored in WorthSheet's tool.
