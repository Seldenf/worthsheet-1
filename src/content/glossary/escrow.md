Say your property taxes are $4,800 a year and homeowners insurance is $1,200 a year. Together that is $6,000, or $500 a month. Your servicer adds $500 to your principal and interest payment, holds the money in escrow and pays the tax and insurance bills for you when they come due.

The CFPB notes that many lenders require escrow so the bills are paid, that escrow can sometimes be required by law, and that taxes and insurance change, so your escrow payment and total monthly payment will change too ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-an-escrow-or-impound-account-en-140/)). Because these items are part of the housing payment, they count toward your [front-end DTI](/glossary/front-end-dti/).

Escrow is also a general term: in a home purchase, an escrow or closing agent can hold funds between signing and closing. The mortgage escrow account described here is the ongoing one managed by the servicer. A loan without escrow still has the costs; you pay them yourself. Include them in the [affordability calculator](/how-much-house-can-i-afford/).
