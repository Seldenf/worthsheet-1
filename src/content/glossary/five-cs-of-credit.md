Each C answers a different lender question:

- **[Character](/glossary/character-credit/):** do you have a record of repaying debts?
- **[Capacity](/glossary/capacity-credit/):** does your income cover the new payment?
- **[Capital](/glossary/capital-credit/):** what savings and net worth do you have?
- **[Collateral](/glossary/collateral/):** what asset secures the loan?
- **[Conditions](/glossary/conditions-credit/):** what is the loan for, and on what terms and in what economy?

The framework is a way to think about lending, and not every lender uses this exact label or list. Fannie Mae's mortgage risk assessment, for example, reviews similar ground (equity investment, credit history, liquid reserves, reliable income and the property as collateral) without using the "five C's" name ([Fannie Mae Selling Guide](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-1-01/comprehensive-risk-assessment)). For a plain explanation aimed at small-business owners, see the [FDIC's Money Smart for Small Business](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-resource-center/mssb-m5-pg.pdf).

Each C has its own page above, so this page stays high level. For how the five fit into a real application, read [what lenders consider when reviewing a loan application](/what-do-lenders-consider-when-reviewing-a-loan-application/).
