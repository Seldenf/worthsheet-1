Example: gross income is $6,000 a month. The proposed housing payment is $1,800, plus a $350 car payment, $150 in minimum credit card payments and a $200 student loan payment: $2,500 in total. Back-end DTI is $2,500 ÷ $6,000 ≈ 41.7%.

Lenders look at this figure most closely because it captures every obligation that competes with the new loan. Fannie Mae counts the housing payment, installment debts that extend beyond 10 months, revolving account payments, lease payments, alimony or child support beyond 10 months and any net rental loss; its maximum ranges from 36% for manual underwriting (up to 45% with compensating strengths) to 50% for loans approved by Desktop Underwriter ([Fannie Mae Selling Guide](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-6-02/debt-income-ratios)). The classic benchmark is 36%.

Common mistakes: using take-home instead of gross pay, and adding living expenses such as food and utilities, which are not debts. The related [front-end DTI](/glossary/front-end-dti/) uses housing costs only; both are explained under [debt-to-income ratio](/glossary/debt-to-income-ratio/).
