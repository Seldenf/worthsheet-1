The term comes from business accounting, where a balance sheet "balances": assets equal liabilities plus equity. For a person, the equity is your net worth, so the same equation applies: assets = liabilities + net worth. For example, $150,000 of assets balance against $110,000 of liabilities plus $40,000 of net worth.

Because it is a snapshot, a personal balance sheet is only accurate on the date shown. It is different from an income statement or budget, which describe money coming in and going out over a period of time. Your balance sheet says nothing about your monthly cash flow.

Lenders rarely use the phrase "personal balance sheet"; they ask for a personal financial statement. The SBA's form, [SBA Form 413](https://www.sba.gov/document/sba-form-413-personal-financial-statement), is a good model: it totals assets and liabilities and computes net worth as total assets minus total liabilities. See a [completed example](/personal-financial-statement-example/) of the format.
