SBA Form 413 is the Personal Financial Statement that each proprietor, general partner, LLC managing member, owner of 20% or more of the business and loan guarantor must complete for an SBA 7(a) or 504 loan, listing personal assets, liabilities, income and contingent liabilities as of a single date.

This guide follows the current edition of the form (05-24, OMB control number 3245-0188, expiring 08/31/2027) from top to bottom. It also flags two places where SBA's own documents currently disagree: how old the numbers may be, and whether a spouse signs. Where anything here differs from your lender's checklist, **your lender's instructions win**, because lenders collect the form for 7(a) and 504 loans. For the short version and a ready-made spreadsheet, see the [SBA Form 413 overview](/sba-form-413/); this article is the line-by-line deep dive.

## Who has to complete SBA Form 413

For a 7(a) loan, 504 loan or surety bond, the form says to complete it for (1) each proprietor; (2) each general partner; (3) the managing member of an LLC; (4) each owner of 20% or more of the equity of the applicant, **including the assets of the owner's spouse and any minor children**; and (5) any person providing a guaranty on the loan.

Return it to the **Lender** processing a 7(a) application, the **Certified Development Company (CDC)** processing a 504 application, or the **Surety Company or Agent** for a bond, not to SBA directly. Other programs use the same form with different rules: Disaster applicants (including sole proprietors, since the revised form) return it to SBA's disaster center, and WOSB and 8(a) participants must use Form 413 itself and, unless legally separated, file a separate form for a spouse. For 7(a) loans you apply through a participating lender, and SBA's free resource partners (SBDCs, SCORE, Women's Business Centers, Veterans Business Outreach Centers) can help you prepare.

## What to gather first

- Statements for every bank, retirement and brokerage account, dated near your "as of" date.
- Mortgage and loan statements with balance, monthly payment and the lender's name and address.
- Property values, purchase dates and original costs for each parcel.
- Life insurance statements showing face amount, **cash surrender value** and beneficiaries.
- Papers for anything you guaranteed or co-signed, and for any pending lawsuit, judgment or tax bill.

## The top of the form: the "current as of" date

The form asks for the date the information is current. SBA's documents give different windows:

- **The form:** the date "must be within 90 days of submission for Disaster or within 120 days of submission for 7(a)/504/SBG/8(a) BD/WOSB." SBA Information Notice 5000-868123 (effective June 13, 2025) confirms 120 days for those programs.
- **SOP 50 10 8.1,** which governs 7(a) and 504 lenders for applications received by SBA on or after October 1, 2026: the lender "must obtain a personal financial statement from all individuals guaranteeing the loan except for Supplemental Guarantors, dated within 90 days of loan approval." Its 7(a) application appendix describes Form 413 or a lender equivalent "signed and dated within 90 days of submission to SBA."

SBA's notice on the SOP adds that it is updating application forms to correspond with the SOP, and that until then lenders must collect the information required by the existing forms and the SOP. So the printed form and the SOP currently describe different windows, and the form may change. Several third-party guides present one rule as settled; the SBA documents do not.

**What to do:** ask your lender which date it requires, then use the shorter of the lender's window and 90 days, dated as close to submission as you can. A statement that is 100 days old at submission can be stale by approval.

## The assets and liabilities columns, line by line

The form says "Omit Cents" on both columns: round to whole dollars.

| Assets line | What to enter | Detail in |
|---|---|---|
| Cash on hand and in banks | Checking balances plus cash held | none |
| Savings accounts | Savings, money-market and CD balances | none |
| IRA or other retirement account | Current balance of each account | Section 5 |
| Accounts and notes receivable | Money others owe you personally, not business receivables | Section 5 |
| Life insurance, cash surrender value only | Cash surrender value, not the death benefit | Section 8 |
| Stocks and bonds | Market value | Section 3 |
| Real estate | Present market value, not cost | Section 4 |
| Automobiles | Present value, with year, make and model | Section 5 |
| Other personal property | Jewelry, art, equipment at realistic resale value | Section 5 |
| Other assets | Anything else of value | Section 5 |

| Liabilities line | What to enter | Detail in |
|---|---|---|
| Accounts payable | Bills currently owed (ask your lender where it wants card balances) | none |
| Notes payable to banks and others | Personal loans and credit lines | Section 2 |
| Installment account (auto) | Car loan balance, plus the **monthly payment** | none |
| Installment account (other) | Other installment loans, plus the **monthly payment** | none |
| Loan(s) against life insurance | Policy loans | Section 8 |
| Mortgages on real estate | Balance on each mortgage | Section 4 |
| Unpaid taxes | Taxes owed | Section 6 |
| Other liabilities | Anything else you owe | Section 7 |

Net worth is **total assets − total liabilities**, and the form says it "must equal total in assets column." A hypothetical tie-out:

| Assets | Amount | Liabilities | Amount |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Cash on hand and in banks | $14,200 | Accounts payable | $1,900 |
| Savings accounts | $38,000 | Notes payable | $25,000 |
| IRA or other retirement | $96,500 | Installment (auto) | $11,300 |
| Life insurance (cash surrender value) | $7,800 | Installment (other) | $4,100 |
| Stocks and bonds | $22,400 | Mortgages on real estate | $238,000 |
| Real estate | $310,000 | Unpaid taxes | $3,600 |
| Automobiles | $18,500 | | |
| Other personal property | $12,000 | | |
| **Total assets** | **$519,400** | **Total liabilities** | **$283,900** |

Net worth: $519,400 − $283,900 = **$235,500**. Check: $235,500 + $283,900 = $519,400, equal to total assets. The $310,000 and $238,000 lines must also match "present market value" and "mortgage balance" in Section 4.

## Section 1: source of income and contingent liabilities

**Source of income** lists salary, net investment income, real estate income and other income. The form says alimony or child support should not be disclosed under "Other Income" unless you want those payments counted toward total income.

**Contingent liabilities** are debts that are not yours today but could become yours. They are not part of total liabilities on the front page, but lenders read them closely. The form lists four lines:

- **As endorser or co-maker:** loans you co-signed or endorsed for someone else.
- **Legal claims and judgments:** suits or judgments pending against you.
- **Provision for federal income tax:** an amount you expect to owe.
- **Other special debt:** for example, a personal guarantee on another person's loan.

See the glossary entry on [contingent liabilities](/glossary/contingent-liabilities/). Leaving this block blank when you have guaranteed something is a common error, and the certification you sign covers the statement being complete.

## Sections 2 to 8 at a glance

| Section | What to list | Ties to the front page |
|---|---|---|
| 2. Notes payable | Noteholder name and address, original and current balance, payment and frequency, how secured or endorsed | Sum of balances = notes payable |
| 3. Stocks and bonds | Shares, security name, cost, market value, date of quotation, total value | Total = stocks and bonds |
| 4. Real estate owned | Each parcel: type, address, purchase date, original cost, present market value, mortgage holder, balance, payment, status | Values = real estate; mortgages = mortgage line |
| 5. Other personal property and assets | Each item, plus any lien holder, amount, terms and delinquency | Retirement, receivables, autos, property, other assets |
| 6. Unpaid taxes | Type, to whom payable, when due, amount, property any lien attaches to | Unpaid taxes |
| 7. Other liabilities | Detailed description | Other liabilities |
| 8. Life insurance held | Face amount, cash surrender value, insurer, beneficiaries | Insurance and policy-loan lines |

Use attachments if you run out of room; each "must be identified as part of this statement and signed."

## Signing, certification and penalties

You first authorize SBA, the lender or the surety to verify your statement. In the certification you "certify under penalty of criminal prosecution that all information on this form and any additional supporting information submitted with this form is true and complete to the best of my knowledge." The block is "to be completed by each person submitting the information requested on this form and the spouse of any 20% or more owner when spousal assets are included."

**The spouse discrepancy.** The form's instructions also say to sign with a "spousal signature, if required." SOP 50 10 8.1 (Section A, Chapter 5) says: "Non-owner spouses are not required to sign the personal financial statement," although the lender must require the spouse's signature on collateral documents, and a spouse owning less than 20% must guarantee the loan in full when both spouses' and minor children's combined ownership is 20% or more. So if you are a 20% owner and your spouse's assets are included, the form's certification covers your spouse; if your spouse is a non-owner, the SOP says no PFS signature is required. Do not assume either applies: follow your lender's checklist.

**Penalties.** The form's notice for 7(a), 504 and surety applicants says a false statement is punishable under 18 U.S.C. §§ 1001 and 3571 (up to five years and/or a $250,000 fine), 15 U.S.C. § 645 (up to two years and/or $5,000) and, for statements to a federally insured institution, 18 U.S.C. § 1014 (up to thirty years and/or $1,000,000). That is a reason to be accurate and complete; supportable estimates are what the form asks for.

## Can I use my own format?

For 7(a), 504 and surety purposes, SBA accepts other formats containing the same data as Form 413, generally including the lender's own equivalent form, most bank-regulator forms and accountant-prepared statements (Information Notice 5000-868123). For **8(a) and WOSB**, you must use Form 413. Ask the lender first, and make sure any spreadsheet includes the Sections 2 to 8 detail, contingent liabilities and the signature.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving off contingent liabilities such as co-signed loans or guarantees.
- A stale "as of" date (see above).
- Totals that do not tie to the schedules in Sections 2 and 4.
- Cost instead of market value for real estate, vehicles and securities, or the death benefit instead of cash surrender value.
- Including cents.
- Mixing business and personal numbers: the form says to complete it with personal information, and to divide jointly owned assets and liabilities appropriately with a spouse or others.
- Forgetting to sign, or to identify and sign attachments.
