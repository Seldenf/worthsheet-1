## Frequently Asked Questions

(Elementor accordion; four items.)

**What is Personal Financial Statement**

A personal financial statement (PFS) is a summary of a person’s financial health and includes a comprehensive list of personal assets (items of value that an individual owns) and liabilities (debts or obligations). A personal financial statement is required in order to receive a loan or mortgage. Download the Worthsheet template for guided instructions to complete a personal financial statement.

**Why do I need to prepare a Personal Financial Statement?**

Preparing a PFS is crucial for several reasons: it is often required when applying for loans or mortgages, helps in planning personal finances, aids in investment decision-making, and is useful for wealth management. It provides a clear picture of your financial situation, which is essential for making informed financial decisions.

**What should be included in a Personal Financial Statement?**

A PFS should include all assets, such as bank accounts, real estate, investments, and personal property valued over a certain amount. It should also list all liabilities, including mortgages, personal loans, credit card debts, and any other financial obligations. Finally, it should reflect the individual’s net worth by subtracting total liabilities from total assets.

**How often should I update my Personal Financial Statement?**

It is advisable to update your PFS annually or whenever there is a significant change in your financial situation. Regular updates ensure that the statement accurately reflects your current financial status, which is essential for effective financial planning and decision-making.
